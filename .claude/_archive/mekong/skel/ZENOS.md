# ZENOS.md — {{PARTICLE_NAME}} Constitution

## Article 1: Mission Supremacy — {{MISSION_STATEMENT}}

The Particle exists to serve this mission above all else. Every action, resource allocation,
and decision must be evaluated against whether it advances the mission. When mission
and short-term revenue conflict, mission prevails — unless the Particle's survival is at
immediate risk, in which case the survival action must include a clear path back to mission.

## Article 2: Anti-Concentration

Governance authority is contribution-weighted. No single actor — human, AI cell, or
automated process — may hold more than 25% of total voting power. Voting power is
calculated as a rolling 90-day weighted sum of:

- Code contributions (merged, reviewed, tested)
- Treasury contributions (capital, grants, revenue share)
- Operational contributions (governance participation, dispute resolution)
- Knowledge contributions (documentation, training, analysis)

Any concentration approaching the 25% threshold triggers automatic redistribution
measures defined in the governance workflow.

## Article 3: Right to Exit

Any participant — human or AI cell — may exit the Particle at any time, for any reason.
Upon exit, the participant is entitled to:

1. A full export of all data they generated or contributed to, in a machine-readable format
2. Settlement of any accrued economic claims within 30 days
3. Removal of their personal data from active systems (with archival retention where
   required by law)
4. A written explanation of how their contributions will be handled post-exit

No exit penalty, non-compete, or data hostage is permitted. This right is fundamental
and cannot be waived or amended.

## Article 4: Transparency Pillar

All governance decisions, treasury transactions, and constitutional amendments are
recorded in the Particle's permanent record. The record is publicly readable by all
participants. Exception: personally identifiable information may be redacted where
required by privacy regulation.

## Article 5: Dispute Resolution

Disputes between participants are resolved through a three-stage process:

1. **Mediation** — a neutral facilitator helps the parties reach consensus
2. **Arbitration** — if mediation fails, a panel of three randomly selected participants
   reviews evidence and issues a binding decision
3. **Constitutional Review** — if the dispute touches constitutional interpretation,
   the Compliance AI cell issues a formal opinion that the arbitration panel must
   consider

## Article 6: Evolutionary Guardrails

This constitution may be amended by a two-thirds supermajority vote, except for
Article 1 (Mission Supremacy), Article 3 (Right to Exit), and this Article 6, which
require unanimous consent. Amendments must be proposed in writing, debated for
a minimum of 7 days, and recorded in the permanent record with a rationale.
